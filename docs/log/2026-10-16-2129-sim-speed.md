# Faster rules, same game (follow-up to the test speed-up)

The owner asked for the rest of the efficiencies from `2026-10-16-2057-test-speed.md`. Playing out matches had become the biggest cost in the quick tier, and it is also how fast the game runs. A profile of a 20-minute, three-computer match pointed at fog of war and the computer's building searches. Every change below keeps the game exactly the same.

- **Fog of war** (`src/sim/vision.ts`): a sight disc is now stamped row by row, each row clipped to the map once and filled in one go, instead of tile by tile with a bounds check each. Was about 17% of a match, now about 6%. The rows are worked out from the same cached disc, so the cache's first-radius-wins quirk is kept.
- **The computer's dock search** (`findDockSpot`): the distance-from-home test and then the shore test (fewer than 3 water tiles beside the spot) now come before `placeProblem`. The search only asks whether there is any problem, and a spot without enough water always has one, so the answer is the same. On land most tiles fail at the shore test, which is far cheaper.
- **`findSpot`** works out each ring tile from its index, in the same reading order, instead of building the ring as an array of pairs. The order was checked against the old code for every radius up to 30 and many start points.
- **`keepClearNear`** (from #365) no longer creates a closure on each call; **`claimants`** tests distance before anything else.
- **Map generation** works out where each town can walk twice, not six times: once for relics and trading posts, once (after the bog iron) for ruins, the wild, landmarks and rare ores. None of those steps changes the ground before looking.

Proof that nothing changed:
- **Matches.** Seven computer matches (continental, sea, lakes, highlands, forest and plains; 2–4 players; all six peoples; 12–25 minutes) give the same checksum every game minute and the same SHA-1 of the full save at the end, before and after. They take 76 s bundled, down from 91 s.
- **Maps.** The 432-map fingerprint from #365 is still identical; 43 ms a map bundled (52 after #365, 70 before it).
- **Tests.** The quick tier passes (614 tests, 1 min 40 s wall, 5 min 43 s CPU, down from 1 min 51 s / 6 min 19 s), and so does the whole slow tier, campaign proofs included (14 files, 59 tests).

Tried and dropped:
- Keeping the stats cache on the player instead of in a `WeakMap`: no gain I could measure.
- Merging the tests that play a match twice: each checks something different (land, sea and planes, passengers through a save, the computer's plans through a save), and comparing two runs needs two runs. Merging them saves about 10 s and loses variety.

Next, if someone wants more: most of what's left is the computer's thinking (`think2` about 28%, `planBuildings` 12%), keeping units apart (`separate`) and finding paths (`findRoute`). In tests, the rules also run about 2× slower than bundled, because vite's module runner reads every imported constant through a getter.
