# A climb nearly paid for keeps its savings (#532)

`ai-ore-sweep` (seed 4) passed on `main` with the Mongol entering Iron and Blood at 27.4 minutes against a 28-minute window, so any unrelated change could flip it. The ore was never the slow part. A timeline (every 6 s) found three stalls, each a general flaw and not a seed-4 one:

- **Army-first spent a banked climb.** A wave came home short (24 of 29), the town held 731 food, 663 gold and 135 stone toward Gunpowder (1000/500/300), and army-first put all of it into soldiers over 90 s; it then saved the whole price again. Now, while army-first holds a climb with a `AI_CLIMB.banked` share (0.6) of the main road's price in hand, that price stays set aside and soldiers come from the surplus. The hold itself is unchanged (`tests/ai-beaten.test.ts` pins it). Not in campaign chapters: with it, the Iron and Gold rival grew strong enough that the proof needed 8 maps instead of 1, so chapters keep the difficulty they were tuned to.
- **A nomad's stone waited on chance.** The Mongols buy stone at the Market only when gold covers the road's gold plus the lot, while the army spends every gold above the road's own. The road's reservation now includes the gold for its stone (`stoneGold`, margin `NOMAD.aiMargin`, the 40 `trade` already used).
- **A Blood-bound town turned pilgrim.** `roadsFrom` lists the Pilgrims before the Warband; with both deeds done the pilgrims won on list order. A town that has chosen the Path of Blood skips the pilgrim turn. And army-first keeps its old priority while a chosen Blood road waits on a deed its army earns (the slain, the plunder), so the keep never starves that deed.
